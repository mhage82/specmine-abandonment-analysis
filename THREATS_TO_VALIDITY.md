# Initial Threats to Validity

The implementation records input-file SHA-256 hashes, table dimensions, processing checks, and output schemas. The dataset revision and dependency versions are documented, and the analysis can be rerun through command-line entry points. Parquet outputs preserve data types for downstream processing, while CSV outputs support inspection. These provisions enable comparison of inputs and regeneration of the exploratory evidence. They support reproducibility but do not substitute for clean reproduction by another team member.

Several threats constrain interpretation. First, the curated sample and broad-census scope limit generalizability. Second, TODOs, placeholders, and unchecked checkboxes are imperfect measures of unfinished work and may occur in templates, examples, or intentionally deferred content. Third, valid timestamps and matching history records do not establish complete historical coverage. Fourth, null-value counts do not capture all semantic missingness, such as blank text, sentinel values, or incorrect but parseable dates. Fifth, specifications are nested within repositories and may share practices, templates, and development context. Subsequent evaluation should address this dependence, including the risk of placing closely related specifications in both training and test sets.

Temporal validity requires additional methodological decisions. The observation cutoff, prediction time, follow-up requirements, and treatment of right-censored specifications must be established before outcome labeling. Snapshot features and history summaries cannot automatically be treated as information available before abandonment. Using outcome-defining indicators or future information as predictors could produce circular evaluation or temporal leakage. The group must also distinguish inactivity due to completion or stability from inactivity associated with abandonment.

The present contribution therefore establishes an executable data foundation and initial empirical evidence for the study. The next research stage should formalize the outcome definitions, assess their validity through inspected examples or annotations, determine temporally appropriate predictors, and evaluate models using a defensible validation design.
